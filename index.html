Abstract

Paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (PAF) is episodic and often asymptomatic, so it frequently escapes the brief window of a clinical visit; anticipating its onset from a wearable stream would open a window for early intervention. We present a patient-aware early-warning scheme for AF built on RR-interval (RRI) sequences, the compact heart-rhythm signal that smartwatches and single-lead patches derive natively from either ECG or PPG. A deliberately small multi-branch convolutional encoder is pretrained with a patient-aware contrastive objective whose positive pairs are restricted to same-patient, same-class segments, so it preserves each individual's own sinus-rhythm baseline rather than collapsing all patients into one shared prototype. On the strictly patient-independent IRIDIA-AF benchmark the frozen representation reaches an AUROC of 0.991 ± 0.005 with 1.6× lower seed-to-seed variance than supervised contrastive learning. Reusing this frozen embedding without any retraining, a per-patient sinus-rhythm-to-AF projection yields a score that crosses its decision boundary a median of 50.9 minutes before annotated onset across eleven calibrated test patients. Deployed on a physical Apple Watch, the model runs in a measured median of 156 µs per window on the Neural Engine and compresses to a 33 KB package at 95.4% accuracy, making both detection and early warning feasible on resource-constrained wearable hardware.

The problem

A silent arrhythmia that hides between heartbeats

Paroxysmal atrial fibrillation starts and stops on its own, often without symptoms, yet it raises stroke risk roughly fivefold. Short clinic tests routinely miss it, and existing wearable models fail on patients they were never trained on, or are simply too heavy to run on-device.

  • Cross-patient generalisation. Standard models overfit the training cohort; sensitivity can drop 12+ points on held-out patients.
  • Lost individuality. Ordinary contrastive learning collapses every patient's unique rhythm into one class prototype.
  • Tight compute budgets. Consumer wearables cannot host server-grade networks.

Patient-aware loss

Positive pairs restricted to the same patient and class, preserving each person's rhythm baseline instead of averaging it away.

Multi-branch encoder

A compact convolutional network with temporal attention pooling reads both ECG-derived RR and PPG-derived pulse intervals.

Geometry insight

Per-patient cohesion, not clean global class separation, is what actually predicts transfer to unseen patients.

Early warning

A lightweight per-patient score tracks drift along a personal sinus-to-AF axis to flag an oncoming episode.

The result

State-of-the-art detection that transfers zero-shot

On IRIDIA-AF our framework reaches an AUROC of 0.991, ahead of supervised-contrastive and cross-entropy baselines and, more importantly, with markedly lower variance across random seeds. Without any fine-tuning, the same encoder transfers to the independent SHDB-AF dataset at AUROC 0.955.
