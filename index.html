03 · Background
What is Atrial Fibrillation?
// cardiac arrhythmia

The heart's rhythm becomes chaotic and irregular.

✓ Normal Sinus Rhythm

Coordinated, Regular

  • Regular, repeating wave pattern with every beat
  • Gaps between beats are nearly the same length
  • Upper and lower chambers work in sync
✗ Atrial Fibrillation

Chaotic, Irregular

  • No organised pattern — upper chambers fire randomly 300-600× per minute
  • Gaps between beats jump around unpredictably
  • Blood pools in the heart → clot risk → stroke
04 · Clinical Impact
Consequences of Undetected AFib
// what happens when AF goes undetected

Untreated AFib triggers a cascade of life-threatening events.

🧠

Ischaemic Stroke — 5× higher risk

Blood clots form in the fibrillating left atrium, travel to the brain, and block cerebral vessels. AF-related strokes are larger and more fatal than strokes from other causes.

❤️

Congestive Heart Failure

Chronic rapid and irregular heart rate progressively weakens the ventricular muscle. Tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy can develop over months of undetected AF.

🧩

Cognitive Decline & Dementia

Recurrent micro-emboli and chronic cerebral hypoperfusion accelerate vascular dementia. AF doubles dementia risk independently of overt stroke episodes.

⚡

Sudden Cardiac Death

AF degenerates into ventricular fibrillation in structurally abnormal hearts. Risk is highest in the first months after AF onset, when patients are typically undiagnosed.

06 · Signal Processing
ECG & R-Peak Detection
// reading the heart's electrical signal

Every heartbeat creates a unique electrical signature — the ECG captures each one.

  • P wave — the upper chambers (atria) fire to push blood down into the ventricles
  • QRS complex — the main pumping chambers (ventricles) squeeze; this is the heartbeat you feel
  • T wave — the heart muscle relaxes and recharges for the next beat
  • R-peak — the sharp spike at the top of each QRS; used as the timestamp of each beat
  • Peak detection software finds these R-peaks automatically in real-time, even on a cheap sensor
// Annotated ECG — one PQRST cycle
● R-peak detected — anchor for beat-to-beat timing
07 · Heart Rate Variability
RR Interval Calculation
// the heartbeat timing signature

The gap between heartbeats — the RR interval — tells us if the rhythm is healthy or not.

  • RR interval — the time (in milliseconds) between two consecutive heartbeat peaks
  • Healthy heart — intervals are similar to each other, with only small natural variation
  • AFib heart — intervals jump around wildly with no predictable pattern, like a shuffled playlist
  • These timing patterns are rich enough for AI to tell SR from AFib — no bulky waveform needed
  • Privacy-first — we only store timing numbers, not your raw heartbeat waveform

08 · Signal Sources
RR & PPG Peak Interoperability
// from hospital ECG to your smartwatch

ECG uses electrical signals. Your watch uses light. But both measure the same heartbeat timing.

⚕ ECG — Electrical

Hospital ECG Sensor

  • Detects tiny electrical signals the heart generates with every beat
  • Produces sharp, clear peaks → very precise beat timing
  • Gold standard in hospitals; what we used for training our model
Training data source: IRIDIA-AF (single-lead ECG, 167 patients)
⌚ PPG — Light Sensor

Smartwatch Sensor (PPG)

  • Shines infrared light into your skin and measures how much bounces back — which changes with each pulse
  • Each blood-volume peak ≈ an ECG R-peak → same interval timing
  • Built into every smartwatch, fitness ring, and pulse oximeter
Deployment target: wearable PPG → same HRV input to our model

The key insight: The beat timing intervals from a smartwatch PPG are essentially the same as those from a hospital ECG. So our AI model — trained on hospital data — runs perfectly on your watch, enabling 24/7 AFib screening without a hospital visit.

10 · System Design
Why Edge Deployment?
// why we run the AI on the watch itself, not in the cloud

Sending data to the cloud is too slow, too risky, and fails exactly when you need it most.

⚡

Real-Time Alerting

No waiting for a server response. The watch detects AFib in under a second — right when you need it, during the episode itself.

🔒

Data Privacy

Your heart data never leaves your device. No server stores it, no company sees it — health data stays private by design, not policy.

🌍

Connectivity-Free

Works on a plane, in a rural village, in a hospital with no Wi-Fi. Internet is optional — critical in low-resource and remote healthcare settings.

💰

Cost Efficiency

No cloud server bills. Once deployed, each additional user costs essentially nothing to monitor — scale to millions of devices for free.

⌚

Wearable-Native

Our model is small enough to run on a smartwatch chip. It only needs heartbeat timing as input — not a complex signal processor or GPU.

🔄

Continuous Monitoring

AFib episodes come and go unpredictably. A 5-minute hospital ECG almost always misses them. 24/7 smartwatch monitoring catches every one.

11 · Methodology
Our Approach
// how our AI model works — step by step

Our model learns your personal rhythm first — then flags when it starts going wrong.

01

Input — 200 Heartbeat Gaps

We feed the model a list of 200 back-to-back beat timing gaps (RR intervals). This tiny snapshot — from an ECG or smartwatch — is all it needs.

02

Pattern Finder — Convolutional Neural Network

A CNN scans the rhythm at multiple scales simultaneously — like reading a paragraph word-by-word, sentence-by-sentence, and paragraph-by-paragraph at once — to spot subtle irregularity.

03

Training Trick — Patient-Aware Learning

When training, we only compare rhythm segments from the same patient. This stops the model from confusing "Patient A with SR" and "Patient B with SR" — each person's normal rhythm is unique.

04

Output — Personal AFib Risk Score

For each new patient, the model defines their personal "normal ↔ AFib" direction and assigns a risk score from 0 (healthy) to 1 (AFib) for every 200-beat window. Continuous, interpretable, real-time.

12 · Interpretability
The Patient-Specific SR→AF Axis
// a personalised risk compass for each patient

We map each patient's "normal" and "AFib" rhythm — then watch which way new beats drift towards.

Training
Encoder learns to separate SR and AF embeddings using patient-aware contrastive loss — keeping each patient's SR distinct.
Axis Definition
Per-patient axis u = (μ_AF − μ_SR) / ‖μ_AF − μ_SR‖ defines a personalised SR→AF direction in frozen latent space.
Inference Score
p_t = (z_t − μ_SR) · u projects any new segment onto [0,1] — a continuous, interpretable measure of AF progression.
