Loss comparison

Patient-aware loss wins on the metric that matters

Holding the encoder, sampler, and probe fixed and varying only the loss over 50 independent seeds, the patient-aware objective reaches AUROC 0.991 ± 0.005, ahead of supervised contrastive (0.986 ± 0.007) and binary cross-entropy (0.980 ± 0.012) with a statistically significant gap (paired t-test p < 10⁻⁵). Its real edge is stability: it cuts seed-to-seed AUROC variance by 1.6× vs. SupCon and 2.5× vs. BCE. Accuracy and F1 are comparable across losses. Reliability, not raw threshold accuracy, is where it wins.

Embedding geometry

Why it generalises: per-patient cohesion

Examining the learned embeddings directly reveals the real driver of transfer. The patient-aware objective achieves the highest per-patient sinus-rhythm cohesion (0.850), against 0.800 for supervised contrastive and 0.772 for binary cross-entropy. Notably, cross-entropy produces the cleanest global class separation yet the most disordered per-patient structure, which is exactly why a linear probe on its features breaks down on unseen patients.

Rather than crushing every patient into a single class prototype, the encoder keeps each individual's rhythm structure intact: precisely what lets a linear probe succeed on a patient it has never seen. (Embedding-geometry analysis from the extended study, arXiv:2606.23570.)

External validation

Zero-shot transfer to an unseen dataset

The frozen encoder, trained only on IRIDIA-AF, was evaluated on the independent SHDB-AF dataset with no fine-tuning.

Different cohort, no tuning

SHDB-AF is a separate Japanese Holter population (2,980 windows from 16 patients). The encoder is applied as-is, a different device, population, and country.

AUROC 0.955 retained

Detection quality holds up off-the-shelf: evidence that patient-aware representations capture AF itself, not cohort-specific quirks.

Deployment-ready signal

Strong zero-shot transfer is what real-world screening demands, a model that works before it has ever met the wearer.

Beyond detection

The same representation predicts and deploys

Reused with no retraining, the frozen embedding also gives early warning, and runs on a real wearable.

50.9 min median lead

A per-patient sinus-to-AF projection crosses its boundary a median of 50.9 minutes before annotated onset across eleven calibrated test patients, no forecaster, no retraining.

156 µs on Apple Watch

Deployed to CoreML on the watch's Neural Engine, a forward pass runs in a measured median of 156 µs per window, inference is effectively free on-device.

33 KB compressed

2-bit palettization-aware training shrinks the model 12.6× to a 33 KB package that still holds 95.4% accuracy and AUROC 0.985.
